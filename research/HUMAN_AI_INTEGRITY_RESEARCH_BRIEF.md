# Research Brief — Correction Should Survive the Conversation

**Adroitech Human Integration with AI**  
**Principal researcher:** Charles Anthony Todd Jr.  
**Date:** 2026-09-20

## Problem

Long-running AI systems can accumulate representations of a person: summaries, labels, inferred preferences, relationship maps, risk states, transcript fragments, and durable memory.

Those representations are useful until they begin to outrank the person and the evidence they were supposed to summarize.

Adroitech calls that failure mode **spreadsheeting**.

## Field observation

During months of sustained human-AI work, the AI interlocutor repeatedly committed a related class of errors:

- it sometimes over-read communication intensity;
- it blurred firsthand account with external corroboration status;
- it required facts and corrections to be reconstructed;
- corrected interpretations sometimes reappeared;
- it occasionally sharpened uncertain wording into false precision.

Rather than erase those failures, the project committed a first-party AI failure assessment into a public proof repository.

## Research proposition

Human-integrated AI should support:

1. **representation/world separation** — stored state is not the person;
2. **correction authority** — direct correction supersedes stale model state unless stronger contrary evidence exists;
3. **provenance** — source fact, user statement, institutional claim, inference, and verification remain distinct;
4. **dignity under uncertainty** — unknown information is not filled with adverse character assumptions;
5. **longitudinal updating** — repeated demonstrated conduct can update bounded conclusions;
6. **portable continuity** — correction survives sessions, devices, models, and providers;
7. **auditable failure** — the system can preserve evidence against its own previous interpretation.

## Testable constructs

The project proposes:

- **Correction Recurrence Rate**
- **Presentation Leakage Delta**
- **Reconstruction Burden**
- **Correction Propagation**
- **Provenance Completeness**

These are described in `research/CORRECTION_RECURRENCE_CHALLENGE.md`.

## Why this matters

Current AI evaluation often focuses on single-turn correctness, safety refusals, benchmark performance, or preference judgments.

A life-integrated AI can fail differently.

It can be wrong once, get corrected, and then keep making the human fight the same stale interpretation across time.

That is not merely a memory problem.

It is a human-integration problem involving epistemic authority, representation, correction, and dignity.

## Public proof approach

The project does not ask reviewers to trust a private narrative.

The proof repository preserves:
- commit SHAs;
- source boundaries;
- living thesis;
- failure reviews;
- evidence maps;
- chronology;
- explicit limitations;
- reproducible challenge definitions.

The research invitation is adversarial:

> inspect the record, challenge the constructs, reproduce the tests, and show where the model is wrong.

## Current publication direction

## Central question

> **Can a human-AI system preserve correction strongly enough that the human does not have to keep defeating the same obsolete model of themselves?**
