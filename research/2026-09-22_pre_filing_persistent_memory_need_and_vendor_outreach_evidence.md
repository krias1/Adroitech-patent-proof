## EVIDENCE A — Google Workspace Support: persistent memory demand and willingness to pay

**Source rail:** Adroitech business Gmail  
**Gmail message ID:** `19e8f12d51224213`  
**Gmail thread ID:** `19e8e9dc9dc8975c`  
**From:** Adroitech LLC <dev.sairk@gmail.com>  
**To:** Google Workspace Support <workspacesupport@google.com>  
**Subject:** `Re: Google Workspace Support 71872339: adroitechsystems.com`  
**Provider timestamp:** 2026-06-03T20:00:35  
**Chronology relation to P1:** approximately 105 days before the 2026-09-16 provisional filing.

### Relevant contemporaneous statements

The inventor told Google directly that he did not want the Ultra-plan media-generation capabilities and instead wanted persistent conversational data / memory:

> "The only thing I need is when I talk to my ... Gemini and the notebook it will remember the shit we talked about an hour ago"

and:

> "I want to persistent data plan where my ... Gemini remembers what the fuck I said 30 minutes ago"

and:

> "20 years of my data and you can't remember 30-minute conversation"

and:

> "I would pay if the system worked"

The same thread also records complaints that Android Auto repeatedly required re-entry of addresses and that Gemini / NotebookLM required repeated restatement of information despite the user's long relationship with Google's data ecosystem.

### Factual propositions supported

This source directly supports that, before P1:

1. the inventor had identified failure of useful continuity / persistent memory as a practical problem;
2. he communicated that problem directly to Google rather than reconstructing it after the patent filing;
3. he distinguished the desired capability from image/video-generation or other compute-heavy subscription features;
4. he expressly wanted persistent data / memory behavior;
5. he expressed willingness to pay for a working implementation; and
6. the problem was experienced across more than one Google product surface.

This record does not by itself establish a patent-law conclusion. Its role is contemporaneous problem-recognition, unmet-user-need, and conception-context evidence to be linked to actual claim limitations and independently researched product capabilities.
