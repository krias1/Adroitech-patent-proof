## Operative limitation

Claim 4 depends from Claim 1 and requires that the durable person-specific world state include an event record having a lifecycle state, with selection of the bounded subset changing according to whether the event record is unresolved, resolved, historical, or superseded.

## Frozen-P1 support

The frozen filing specification's Relationship/Event disclosure establishes that Relationship and Event objects may have lifecycle states including `current`, `historical`, `unresolved`, `resolved`, `pending`, `disputed`, `inactive`, and `superseded`. It further establishes that record existence does not require permanently high relevance and that dynamic relevance may decrease after resolution while the record remains preserved for history and provenance.

The candidate-claim support chart identifies Claim 4 support at DD §§4 and 8 with FIGS. 2 and 5. The claim is therefore controlled to the disclosed lifecycle-driven relevance/activation mechanism operating in the bounded current-world-slice selection architecture inherited from Claim 1.

## Breadth fences

1. **No deletion requirement.** Resolution or supersession does not require deletion. The frozen disclosure expressly preserves records for history/provenance while reducing current activation/relevance.
2. **No mandatory numeric weighting algorithm.** Claim 4 is controlled to a change in bounded-subset selection caused by lifecycle state; it does not require an undisclosed mathematical scoring formula.
3. **No universal exclusion rule.** A resolved, historical, or superseded event is not necessarily excluded from every future world slice. Historical/provenance context can make preserved records relevant again.
4. **No later lifecycle semantics backfilled into P1.** Later implementation details may corroborate or improve later claims but do not expand the frozen priority boundary.
5. **Inherited architecture matters.** The support finding is for Claim 4 as dependent from Claim 1's bounded-selection/current-world-slice mechanism, not a claim to lifecycle labels in the abstract.
